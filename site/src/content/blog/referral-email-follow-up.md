Silence after a referral request almost never means no. It means the email arrived
during a meeting, got triaged into "later", and later hasn't happened. A nudge is
useful. A third nudge is a decision you've made for them.

## The schedule

**Nudge one: three working days after the original.** Long enough that they've had a
real chance, short enough that the role is still open and the thread is still in their
inbox.

**Nudge two: a week after that.** Framed as the last one, because it is.

**Then stop.** Not "wait a month and try again". Stop.

Three working days matters more than three days. An email sent Thursday should be
nudged Tuesday, not Sunday.

## Always the same thread

Reply to your own message. Never start a new one.

A new email makes them reconstruct who you are. A reply carries the original underneath
it, so the context is one scroll away, and it signals you're being persistent rather
than scattershot. It also keeps your two follow-ups visibly *two* rather than looking
like three separate strangers.

## What a nudge says

Short. No new arguments. No guilt.

> Hi Priya, bumping this once in case it got buried. Still interested in the frontend
> role, and still completely fine to be told no.

The second and final one adds exactly one thing — that it's the last:

> Last nudge from me on this, then I'll stop. If the timing is bad or it's not a fit,
> no explanation needed.

That closing line isn't politeness for its own sake. It gives them a costless exit,
and a costless exit is often what produces a reply — sometimes a redirect to the right
person, which is worth nearly as much as a yes.

## What a nudge must not do

**Don't restate your qualifications.** They either read the first email or they didn't.
Repeating it makes the thread longer and the ask heavier.

**Don't add urgency that isn't yours to add.** "The role closes Friday" is the company's
deadline, not theirs, and it reads as pressure.

**Don't switch channels.** If email went unanswered, a LinkedIn DM and a Twitter reply
don't add a signal — they add a pattern. One channel, two nudges.

**Don't apologise for following up.** "Sorry to bother you again" invites them to agree
that you're a bother.

## When to stop early

Stop after one nudge if the posting comes down, if you get an automated rejection from
the company, or if they reply with anything that isn't a yes. A soft no is a no; treating
it as an opening is how a warm contact becomes a cold one.

## Why two

Two is where the curve flattens. The first nudge recovers the emails that were simply
missed, which is most of them. The second catches the genuinely busy. A third only
reaches people who have already decided, and the cost of annoying someone at a company
you want to work at outlasts the application.
