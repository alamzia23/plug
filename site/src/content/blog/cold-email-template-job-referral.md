title: "Cold email template for a job referral (that people actually answer)"
description: "Three templates — alumni, same team, recruiter — plus the subject lines that get opened and the follow-up that doesn't annoy."

Templates are a starting point, not a script. The parts you must change are the ones
that prove you meant it: the connection, and the reason for this specific role. Everything
else can stay.

## Subject lines

Keep it under seven words, lowercase, no urgency tricks. These work because they say
exactly what the email is:

- `referral for the [role] role?`
- `[school] grad, applying to [team]`
- `quick ask about [req number]`

Avoid "Opportunity", "Quick question" and anything with an exclamation mark. They read
as sales.

## Template 1 — you share something

> Hi [name] — I saw you're on the [team] team at [company]. I'm also a [school] grad
> ([year]), now [role] at [current company].
>
> I'm applying to [role title], [link]. It lines up with what I've been doing: [one
> concrete thing — a system you built, a number you moved].
>
> Would you be willing to refer me? Happy to send a resume or anything else useful.
>
> Either way, no problem at all.

## Template 2 — no connection, same function

> Hi [name] — I don't think we've met. I'm a [role] at [company], and I'm applying to
> [role title] on your team ([link]).
>
> I'm reaching out to you rather than a recruiter because you'd actually know whether
> the work matches: I've spent the last [n] years on [specific area].
>
> If you're open to referring me, I'd appreciate it. If not, no worries — and if there's
> someone better to ask, I'd take the pointer.

The "if not, who else" line is the highest-value sentence in a cold ask. A redirect costs
them nothing and is worth almost as much as a yes.

## Template 3 — recruiter

> Hi [name] — I'm applying to [role title] ([req]). [One line: years, function, the
> closest thing you've done to this job.]
>
> Is that a profile worth putting in front of the hiring manager?

Shorter on purpose. Recruiters skim, and they answer questions faster than requests.

## The follow-up

One, after three working days, on the same thread. Never a new email.

> Hi [name] — bumping this once in case it got buried. Still keen on [role], and still
> happy to be told no.

If nothing comes back after another week, stop. Two nudges is the limit before you're a
problem.

## What to change every time

- the connection line
- the one concrete thing about your work
- the role and the link

If you can send the same email to two people without editing those three, neither will
reply.
