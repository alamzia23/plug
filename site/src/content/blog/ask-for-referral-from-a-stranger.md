description: "A stranger will refer you if you make it cheap to say yes. Here's the structure that works, and the four mistakes that kill it."

Most referral requests fail for the same reason: they ask the reader to do work. Work
means reading a long message, figuring out which role you mean, deciding whether you're
any good, and then finding the internal form. Four jobs. So they leave it for later, and
later never comes.

A referral from a stranger is a small favour, not a big one. Most companies pay a bonus
for a hire, referring costs about ninety seconds, and nobody's reputation is on the line
for passing along a name. Your job is to make those ninety seconds obvious.

## The structure

**One line on who you are.** Not your career. The version that matters for this job.

> I'm a frontend engineer, four years, mostly design systems at a fintech.

**One line on why them.** Specific and true. Shared school, shared previous employer,
a talk they gave, a post they wrote. If you have nothing, say you found them because
they work on the team — that's honest and still better than pretending.

**The role, with a link.** Never "any openings". Name the req.

**The ask, sized small.** You are not asking them to vouch for you. You are asking them
to submit a name.

> Would you be up for dropping my name in the referral form? Happy to send anything
> that makes it easier.

**An exit.** One clause. It raises reply rates because it removes the guilt of saying no.

> Totally fine if you'd rather not — I know you don't know my work yet.

That's five sentences. Under 120 words. It should be readable on a phone without
scrolling.

## The four things that kill it

**Attaching your resume unasked.** It turns a ninety-second favour into homework. Offer
it; let them ask.

**"Any openings?"** Now they have to go find the job. You've handed them your task.

**Flattery that isn't specific.** "I love your work" reads as a template, because it
usually is. If you can't name the thing, cut the sentence.

**Following up more than twice.** One nudge after three days, one final after a week,
then stop. Silence is an answer, and people who feel chased don't refer.

## Who to ask

In order: someone who shares a specific thing with you (school, previous employer,
hometown), then someone on the actual team, then a recruiter. Recruiters are last, not
first — they get the most of these and have the least incentive to pass one along.

Skip anyone more than two levels above the role. A VP forwarding your email to a
recruiter is worth less than a peer clicking the referral button.

## When

Tuesday to Thursday, morning their time. Within a week of the posting going up — after
that the pipeline is full and a referral carries less weight.
